problem: "A friend who builds mobile apps was making DigiSkills, a free app that teaches children online safety and is built for students on low-end phones. It needed a mark a child would want to open, that still reads on a small, low-resolution screen, and that never looks cheap just because the app is free."
constraints:
  - "Reads as a 48 px app tile: no thin strokes or fine detail"
  - "Works in a single colour, on cheap displays and in print"
  - "Flat, high-contrast colour that survives budget screens"
  - "Text and controls clear WCAG AA contrast"
  - "Free for everyone, without looking like charity"
approach: "The mark joins the oldest symbol of learning to the newest: an open book, with three pixel squares lifting off its pages. I kept it a bold, solid silhouette so it holds at any size. Then I tested it in colour, five directions first and eight more after, and chose indigo and amber: a deep indigo for trust that also suits a dark, battery-friendly mode, and a warm amber for the pixels. I tried a lighter outline version inside a rounded tile too, but the solid book stayed readable far smaller, so the solid mark became the icon."
outcome: "The final system is an indigo book with amber pixels on light backgrounds, and a white book on an indigo tile for the app icon, which holds its shape from 140 px down to 28 px. I also designed the app's screens in a UI/UX study. The app has been submitted to the App Store and Google Play."
