problem: "A friend who builds mobile apps was making DigiSkills, a free app that teaches children online safety and is built for students on low-end phones. It needed a mark a child would want to open, that still reads on a small, low-resolution screen, and that never looks cheap just because the app is free."
constraints:
  - "Reads as a small app tile: no thin strokes or fine detail"
  - "Works in a single colour, on cheap displays and in print"
  - "Flat, high-contrast colour that survives budget screens"
  - "Text and controls clear WCAG AA contrast"
  - "Free for everyone, without looking like charity"
approach: "I drew the mark in Illustrator: an open book, three pages deep on each side, with three pixel squares lifting off it. One half is drawn and the other is its mirror. The pixels shrink as they rise, at 1 : 2/3 : 1/2, and the largest sits on the spine, so they read as lifting off the page. In one morning I tried it three ways: solid, as line art, and in indigo with see-through pixels that overlap. The final keeps the first draft's shape and separate pixels, takes the indigo from the third, and adds amber, chosen from five colour directions in a colour study."
outcome: "The finished system has a full-colour mark with its back pages at 80% opacity for depth, a flat one-colour version, a white knockout, and an app icon with the white book on an indigo tile. Rendered straight from the vector, the icon keeps the book and its three pixels down to 24 px. I also designed the app's screens in a UI/UX study, and the app has been submitted to the App Store and Google Play."
