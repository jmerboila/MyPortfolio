The Person Behind the Mark

A high-contrast serif monogram fuses his initials — J and M — into one elegant, vertical signature, placing his name in the lineage of great initial marks (LV, CC, YSL) where two letters carry an entire reputation.

04 — Concept
Anatomy of the Monogram

Where the J meets the M.

i

Interlocking letterforms

The descending tail of the J threads through the heart of the M, fusing two initials into a single graphic unit rather than two letters sitting side by side. That fusion is what elevates a lettermark into a true monogram.

ii

High-contrast Didone serif

Dramatic thick-to-thin stroke modulation and crisp bracketed serifs evoke heritage, fashion and editorial prestige — the visual language of luxury houses.

iii

Vertical, portrait balance

The tall composition reads like a wax seal or a maker's stamp — perfect for spine prints, social avatars and embossing, where a compact, centred mark outperforms a wide wordmark.

05 — Construction
Built on a Grid

Symmetry, measured.

A central vertical axis, a shared cap height and baseline, and matched side bearings govern the stem weights, serif lengths and the exact point where the two letters cross — so the monogram rebuilds identically at any scale.

06 — Spacing
Clearspace & Minimum Size

Room to be refined.

Keep an exclusion zone of ½× the monogram height on every side. The mark's elegance depends on isolation — never crowd it with type or imagery.

11 — Usage
Do's & Don'ts

Guard the signature.

✓ Do
  • ✓ Use Ink or Gold on clean, generous backgrounds.
  • ✓ Reverse to white or gold foil on dark fields.
  • ✓ Preserve the ½× clearspace and central balance.
  • ✓ Let the monogram stand alone — it's the signature.
✕ Don't
  • ✕ Stretch, skew or rotate the monogram.
  • ✕ Recolour it outside the brand palette.
  • ✕ Add outlines, shadows or busy gradients.
  • ✕ Place it on low-contrast or cluttered imagery.
